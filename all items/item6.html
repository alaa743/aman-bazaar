Benedetto Antelami: Praise of Gravity

Benedetto Antelami was a pioneering Italian sculptor and architect of the Romanesque period (c. 1150–1230). The Concept: The "praise of gravity" refers to his departure from the "weightless," flat figures of earlier medieval art. Style: He gave stone a sense of physical mass and monumental weight, making figures feel grounded and human. Key Work: His masterpiece, The Deposition in Parma Cathedral, portrays the sorrow of Christ’s descent with a profound sense of physical and emotional gravity. Significance: He used the natural weight of stone to express the human condition—labor, suffering, and a tangible presence in the real world.

These statues belong to the San Agustín Archaeological Park in Colombia, home to the largest collection of religious monuments and megalithic sculptures in South America.

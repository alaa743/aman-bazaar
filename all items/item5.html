Samarra Stucco Reliefs

Origin: Samarra, Iraq (Abbasid Caliphate), 9th century CE. Context: Used to decorate the vast walls of palaces (like Jawsaq al-Khaqani) in the temporary Abbasid capital. The Three Styles: Style A: Traditional, deeply carved floral and vine motifs. Style B: More stylized and abstract with less background depth. Style C (The "Beveled Style"): The most famous; features repeating, abstract, "S-shaped" patterns with slanted edges. Legacy: Represented a major shift toward abstraction in Islamic art, heavily influencing future arabesque and geometric designs.

Parthenon Marbles (Elgin Marbles)

Origin: Athens, Greece, c. 447–438 BCE. Component: Architectural sculptures from the Parthenon temple, created under Phidias. Subjects: The frieze depicts the Panathenaic Procession; metopes show mythical battles; pediments show scenes of the goddess Athena. Location: Mostly in the British Museum, London; others in the Acropolis Museum, Athens.
